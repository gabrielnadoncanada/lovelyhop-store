The Montessori approach has nothing to do with room size. It rests on one idea: **the child should be able to do things on their own**. Take an activity, use it, put it back. A well-planned one-square-metre corner beats a cluttered playroom.

## Start by taking things away

Before buying anything, remove. A child facing thirty toys does not really choose any of them. Keep **six to ten activities** visible and store the rest in a closet. Every two or three weeks, swap two or three of them.

## Everything at the child's height

Get down on your knees and look at the room through their eyes. What they cannot see or reach does not exist for them. Low shelves, a table and chair their size and a few hooks at their height do most of the work.

## A place for everything

Each activity has its tray or basket, and its fixed spot. The child quickly learns where things go, and tidying up becomes something they do on their own. Avoid big toy chests where everything gets mixed up: children dig through them, they do not choose.

## Furniture that does several things

In a small space, every piece of furniture has to earn its place. A table that is a sensory bin in the morning, a play table in the afternoon and a low table at snack time replaces three pieces. With the lids closed, it becomes a flat surface that takes up no more room than a coffee table.

A wall activity board frees up the floor; an art table with storage keeps paper and crayons within reach, without spilling onto the kitchen table.

## Define the space without walling it off

A rug is enough to say "this is my corner". Place it near natural light if possible, away from walkways. The child quickly understands that this is where they work, and that things go back in their place before leaving.

## Let the corner evolve

An 18-month-old and a 4-year-old do not have the same needs. Choose furniture that adjusts in height and changes use, rather than replacing everything at each stage. The corner grows with the child, without taking up more room.
