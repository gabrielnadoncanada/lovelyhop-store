Sensory play has a bad reputation for one reason: the cleanup. Yet most messes come from the setup, not the child. A bin that is too full, too high, with no clear limits, and the rice ends up in the living room. Set up well, the same bin becomes a calm moment a child can stretch out for a long time.

## Choose the right container

A good sensory bin is **wide and shallow**. The child should see the bottom and reach it without leaning over the edge. A tall, narrow bin forces them to plunge their arm in, and that is when the elbow tips everything over.

Height matters as much as the bin. Standing, the rim should reach the child's waist; seated, their belly button. Too low and they lean in and spill; too high and they lift their arms and the filler slides down their sleeves. That is why a height-adjustable sensory table grows with the child from age 1 to 6.

## Measure the filler

The most useful rule fits in one sentence: **a third of the bin, no more**. That is enough to bury hands, fill a cup and empty it. Beyond that, every movement spills over.

Start with less, then add. A child discovering a new material handles it more vigorously in the first few minutes; after that, the movements settle and become precise.

## Three rules that keep the floor clean

1. **The filler stays in the bin.** Say it simply, before starting. If it leaves the bin on purpose, the activity stops and you try again later, without drama.
2. **Tools stay above the bin.** A spoon waved over the floor means rice everywhere. Show the movement: fill and pour above the bin.
3. **We tidy up together.** A small dustpan and brush within the child's reach are part of the activity. Picking up what fell is a practical life skill, not a punishment.

<Callout title="Safety">
Before age 3, avoid materials and objects small enough to swallow (dried beans, marbles, small toys). Water needs constant supervision, even when shallow. An adult always stays close by.
</Callout>

## Protect the floor, simply

An old tablecloth, a shower curtain or a picnic blanket under the table does the job. At the end, fold it toward the centre and tip what it caught back into the bin. For water, a towel underfoot and a waterproof apron take care of it.

## Store it for next time

Dry fillers keep for a long time in a sealed container. Having **two bins** changes everything: while one dries after water play, the other is ready for rice or sand. The lids turn the table into a flat play surface as soon as the activity is over.

## In short

A wide, shallow bin at the right height, filled a third of the way, with three rules said ahead of time. That is all it takes for sensory play to become what it should be: a moment of focus, not a chore.
