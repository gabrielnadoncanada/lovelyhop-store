Between ages 1 and 3, children learn mostly with their hands and their mouth. Sensory activities give them a place to touch, pour, squeeze and observe. No special equipment needed: your kitchen already holds half of it.

The ideas below are sorted by age and by **mess level** (★ low, ★★ medium, ★★★ have a towel ready).

<Callout title="Safety">
At this age, everything ends up in the mouth. Choose materials that are edible or too big to swallow, and stay next to the child for the whole activity. No water beads, dried beans or small parts before age 3.
</Callout>

## From 12 months

1. **Warm water and sponges** ★★★ A little water, two sponges in different colours. Squeeze, watch it drip, start again.
2. **Dry oats** ★★ Rolled oats in a bin, a big spoon and a bowl. Soft to the touch, and harmless if a little ends up in the mouth.
3. **Cold cooked pasta** ★★ Cooked, cooled fusilli, slippery and soft. Grab them, drop them.
4. **Tissue paper to scrunch** ★ A few coloured sheets to crumple, tear and put away in a box. The sound is half the fun.

## Around 18 months

5. **Pouring water** ★★★ Two cups and a funnel. Pouring from one container to another takes coordination that improves week after week.
6. **Ice and small toys** ★★ Large figurines frozen in ice cubes. Watch them melt and free them with warm water.
7. **Cereal and a spoon** ★★ Large ring-shaped cereal, a spoon and a silicone muffin tray to fill one cup at a time.
8. **Shapes on a light surface** ★ Translucent shapes on a soft light. Stack them, layer the colours, watch what changes.

## Around age 2

9. **Moulding sand** ★★ Slightly damp sand, small moulds and a shovel. Fill, pack, turn out.
10. **Washing toys** ★★★ A bin of soapy water, a brush and a few "dirty" plastic toys. As much a practical life activity as a sensory one.
11. **Coloured rice and treasures** ★★ Uncooked rice (from two and a half, with supervision) and a few large objects hidden inside to find.
12. **Sorting by colour** ★ Balls or large pompoms to sort into two coloured bowls. Name the colours as you play.

## Keep the setup simple

One activity at a time, on a surface at the child's height. A bin filled a third of the way, only a few tools, and one rule: the filler stays in the bin. For details, see our guide [How to set up a mess-free sensory bin](/en/guides/mess-free-sensory-bin).

## Observe rather than direct

The adult's role is mostly to prepare, then step back a little. A child pouring the same cup ten times is not bored: they are learning. Step in for safety, and to tidy up together at the end.
